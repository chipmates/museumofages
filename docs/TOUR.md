# A tour of Museum of Ages

Eleven pictures of the da Vinci wing as it stands on [museumofages.org](https://museumofages.org) in October 2026. They were taken in the museum itself, on a desktop and on a phone, in English and once in German. The works you see in them are listed with their credits [at the end](#credits).

## Three layers of text

A stop has a line you read first, a drawer with a few more lines, and a record that gives the grade and names the sources.

<p align="center">
  <img src="../.github/assets/tour/03-drawer.webp" alt="The Mona Lisa hangs in a wooden frame on a dark wall, with a drawn copy of the Battle of Anghiari and two smaller Madonnas to its left. Below, the stop's drawer is open with five short lines: what hangs here is a reproduction, the painting itself is in Paris, a clerk in Florence wrote in the margin of a book in October 1503, his note names Lisa del Giocondo, and the picture's name today comes from a book of 1550." width="100%" /><br/>
  <em>The drawer at the Mona Lisa's stop.</em>
</p>

<p align="center">
  <img src="../.github/assets/tour/04-record.webp" alt="A record panel over the darkened mechanism hall, with the tabs This station, This room and The wing. It is graded Reconstructed and headed The mechanism hall, one: flight, and it says the aerial screw was rebuilt from one page of Manuscript B and that no flight of his own is documented." width="100%" /><br/>
  <em>The record at the aerial screw. Its grade is reconstructed, and it says that no flight of his is documented.</em>
</p>

## A close look

<p align="center">

## A machine in motion

<p align="center">
  <img src="../.github/assets/tour/06-aerial-screw.webp" alt="A wooden aerial screw with a spiral linen sail on a round platform, caught mid turn on a pale tiled floor. A small framed facsimile page of his sketch stands at the right. The caption reads The mast takes the turn and carries it up into the spiral, and the bar below says he wrote that air holds a screw as wood does." width="100%" /><br/>
  <em>The aerial screw, built in code after one page of Manuscript B, turning step by step. The page itself stands beside it in an 1883 facsimile.</em>
